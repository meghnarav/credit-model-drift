### Table 02: Subgroup Fairness & Error Decomposition ([`results/Table_02_subgroup_fairness_breakdown.csv`](file:///Users/meghna/Documents/Studio/github/credit-model-drift/results/Table_02_subgroup_fairness_breakdown.csv))
*Subgroup sample sizes: Thin-File ($n = 688$ at $T_0$, $n = 460$ at $T_1$), Thick-File ($n = 2174$ at $T_0$, $n = 923$ at $T_1$), Other ($n = 3012$ at $T_0$, $n = 1676$ at $T_1$).*

- **Thin-File Recall Collapse:**
  - Logistic Regression Recall drops from $53.9\%$ ($T_0$) to $25.7\%$ (Drifted $T_1$), recovering marginally to $27.6\%$ upon retraining.
  - XGBoost Recall drops from $59.8\%$ ($T_0$) to $28.1\%$ (Drifted $T_1$, a $-31.7\%$ drop), recovering to $43.3\%$ upon retraining.
- **Disproportionate False Negative Rate (FNR) Surge:**
  - For thin-file applicants under XGBoost drift, FNR surges from $40.2\%$ to $71.9\%$ (over $71\%$ of creditworthy thin-file applicants are erroneously denied!).
  - For thick-file applicants under XGBoost drift, FNR increases from $23.2\%$ to $50.5\%$.
  - Disparity in FNR under drift: $+21.4\%$ higher denial rate for thin-file applicants.

## 3. Discovered Limitations & Failures Found

1. **Covariate Shift Resampling vs. Real Macroeconomic Time-Series:** The study simulates drift using biased subsampling on `NetFractionRevolvingBurden`. While this captures non-stationary utilization stress, it does not substitute for longitudinal panel datasets spanning multiple interest-rate hiking cycles.
2. **Sample Size Constraints in Local Recourse Batches:** Generating counterfactuals via optimization for large cohorts is computationally expensive. When evaluating subsets with $n < 30$, two-proportion asymptotic z-tests lose statistical power, necessitating non-parametric bootstrap intervals and Fisher's exact tests.
3. **Attribution Stability Divergence:** While linear models maintain high feature attribution stability ($\rho > 0.98$), tree ensembles experience notable rank-order degradation ($\rho = 0.528$). Regulators cannot assume SHAP explanations remain consistent across model retraining cycles.
4. **Action Cost Disparity:** Thin-file borrowers face higher normalized $L_1$ distances to regain approval under post-drift decision surfaces, demonstrating an unequal burden of adaptation.
